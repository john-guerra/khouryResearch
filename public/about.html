About this project

A short story about how the Khoury Researcher Connections viz came together. Khoury Research Day · May 4, 2026.

What this is

Each year, Khoury College runs a "Research Day" where faculty and students share a one-slide summary of what they can give (offer collaborators) and what they want to get (need help with). It's a thoughtful format — but the slides live in a PowerPoint deck that most people never re-open after the event.

This tool turns that deck into a persistent, interactive directory. It surfaces three different kinds of connections between researchers as toggleable layers:

  • Give → Get matches — when one person's "give" semantically matches another's "get". A marketplace view that crosses topic clusters.
  • Topic similarity — researchers who think about similar things, based on their full profile.
  • Shared keywords — researchers whose self-reported (and auto-extracted) keywords overlap.

How it was built

The code was written by Claude Code (Anthropic's coding agent) on behalf of John A. Guerra Gómez, in a single working session — about an afternoon, end-to-end. John drove the direction: framing the problem, choosing between design alternatives, reviewing the visualization, and asking for the refinements that shaped the final feel. Claude wrote the parser, the embedding pipeline, the D3 frontend, and this page. The flow followed a deliberate path:

  • Brainstorming. First we explored what kinds of connections to surface (similarity vs. complementarity vs. shared keywords) and who the audience was. We landed on "all three, as toggleable layers, in a persistent department directory."
  • Planning. Wrote a focused v1 plan: PPTX-only ingestion, Python preprocessing, a static D3 frontend, deferred auto-enrichment to v2. Constraints over features.
  • Implementation. Built the Python pipeline (PowerPoint parsing → sentence-transformer embeddings → similarity matrices → graph JSON), then the D3 force-directed frontend. End-to-end in well under a day.
  • Iteration. Several rounds of small, sharp refinements — animations on layer toggle, edge labels on demand, scented filter chips with sparkbars, a viewport-scale technique to keep nodes visible, collapsible side panels for mobile.

What was hard (and what wasn't)

The fastest part was the pipeline. python-pptx reads slides cleanly, sentence-transformer embeddings are stable enough that "give-mean vs. get-mean" cosine similarity gave honestly meaningful matches on the first try — including a delightful one where the system correctly paired "AI Coding" (a give) with "Vibe Coding expertise" (a get) across two researchers who hadn't met.

The slowest parts were the surprises:

  • An SVG <clipPath> bug that silently hid every photo because userSpaceOnUse evaluated the clip in the SVG root frame instead of the per-node translated frame. Switching to CSS clip-path: circle(50%) on the SVG image was a one-line fix once we figured out what was going on.
  • A version mismatch between the latest KeyBERT and sentence-transformers 5.x. Replaced KeyBERT with a 25-line equivalent (CountVectorizer + cosine similarity + MMR for diversity). Less dependency surface, just as good for our scale.
  • "Visualization" and "information visualization" don't share Jaccard tokens when treated as multi-word phrases. Tokenizing keywords down to individual content words made the keyword layer actually work.

One small infovis trick

The graph is wrapped in two d3 scales (xScale, yScale) that map simulation coordinates to screen coordinates with a margin. The simulation runs unconstrained; the scales adapt their domain to the bounding box of the current layout (with a soft lerp to avoid jitter). This guarantees every researcher stays inside the canvas with their photo and label fully visible — no matter how the layout drifts when you toggle layers, drag a node, or resize the window. It's a small piece of code with a disproportionate UX payoff.

What's next

v1 covers a single year of Research Day attendees. The natural follow-ups, in order of ambition:

  • Auto-enrichment from public sources (Khoury directory, DBLP, Google Scholar) so non-attendees show up too.
  • A 2D embedding/UMAP map as a secondary view, once the corpus grows past ~80 researchers (it's too unstable at N=24).
  • Profile claiming so people can keep their bullets, photos, and keywords up to date without waiting for the next Research Day.
